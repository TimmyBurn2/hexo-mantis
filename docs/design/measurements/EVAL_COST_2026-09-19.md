## (i) Who consumes `best_model` — by symbol, verify-at-HEAD

Self-play serves the LEARNER's live weights, never the promoted net. `mantis.run` hands
`trainer.model` to `WorkerPool(model=trainer.model, …)` (`run.py`), which passes the same module to
`InferenceServer(model, …)` (`selfplay/pool.py`); `ActorSync.maybe_sync(train_step)`
(`train/actor_sync.py`) is driven once per training step by the coordinator and, on every
`train.actor_sync_cadence_steps` boundary (2 in run7/run8 — 4 330 `actor_sync` rows over run8's
8 659 steps), calls `pool.sync_inference_weights(trainer.inference_state_dict())` →
`pool_hooks.sync_inference_weights` → `InferenceServer.load_state_dict_safe`, which
`load_state_dict`s the learner's tensors INTO the module the server holds (the self-copy
CARD-SERVER-OWNED-COPY names). `run.py`'s own comment at the seam: "the actor's weights come from
the learner on a cadence and NEVER from a gate decision", pinned by
`tests/train/test_actor_sync_isolation.py`. Promotion (`eval/promote.py::apply_gate_decision`, the
ONE call site) writes exactly three things: the evaluated candidate snapshot's tensors into the
in-memory `resolved_anchor.best_model`, that module to `checkpoints/best_model.pt` (+ `.provenance.json`
`{step, run_id, encoding, promoted}`, `save_best_model_atomic`), and `resolved_anchor.best_model_step`.
Its consumers, every one outside the self-play loop: (1) the NEXT gate round's anchor —
`eval/pipeline.py::_build_round_spec` writes `resolved_anchor.best_model` to
`eval_spool/<round>_best.pt`, the child's `best_snapshot`; verified on run8's spool by tensor hash:
`r000002_6000_best.pt` == `r000001_3000_candidate.pt` (50 tensors, `9dfe8ef8…`), i.e. the promoted
r1 candidate IS r2's opponent; (2) the resume path — `load_best_model_resilient(best_model.pt)`
rebuilds the anchor at boot (`run.py`, both launch arms); (3) the next RUN's warm start — run8's
parent was run7's `best_model` at 42k (R356(c)); (4) the sealbot rung plays the CANDIDATE, not the
anchor, so it does not consume it either. **Promotion selects nothing in the loop**: the gate is a
comparator of the learner's snapshot against a moving anchor; its only in-loop effect is where
that anchor sits for the next comparison. R361(c)'s last sentence therefore applies. One stale
docstring found on the way: `pool_hooks.sync_inference_weights` says "a promoted state_dict"; the
argument is the learner's (`trainer.inference_state_dict()`); reworded on contact.

## (iii) The GSPRT's long end — observed rate vs H0, and what H0 = 0.52 does

The rule (`eval/sequential.py`): van den Bergh's normalised-t LLR over PAIR scores, Wald bounds
`(log(β/(1−α)), log((1−β)/α))` = (−2.25, +2.89), 16 pairs then every 8 to 104, the LLR's SIGN at
the maximum. Its zero-drift point is the MIDPOINT (μ0 + μ1)/2 = **0.57**, not H0: a candidate whose
true pair-score mean sits near 0.57 drifts nowhere and runs to `max_pairs`. run7's data agree —
the four rounds that ran longest had observed rates 0.601 (r6, max), 0.562 (r24, max), 0.503
(r11, 72 pairs), 0.518 (r21, 56 pairs); the 13 rejects with observed rates ≤ 0.50 stopped in 16–48
pairs. The 17 rejects' observed rates: 0.328, 0.391, 0.406, 0.427, 0.458, 0.461, 0.469, 0.475,
0.477, 0.484, 0.487, 0.495, 0.495, 0.500, 0.503, 0.512, 0.518 — eleven within 0.06 of H0.

## The pre-stated directions (R361(c)), each against its number

- **Gate cadence 15 000.** run7 ran 25 rounds in 83 482 steps (one per 3 000, two skipped busy);
  at 15 000 it would have run 5, ≈ 9 h of rounds instead of 50 — ESTIMATE ≈ 11–12 h of run7's wall
  returned. The reading is unaffected (R361(a): the strix points ARE the 15k points). The anchor
  then moves at most once per 15k; the census says that costs the loop nothing (§(i)).
- **Gate sims 64 (candidate vs anchor).** The gate game is the unit that costs: 38–40 s at 256/256
  over 91–93 plies with 1–14 games per round at the 256-ply cap, against the rung's 8–9 s at 256
  vs sealbot's ≈ 0 and the strix cell's ≈ 14–17 s at 256/256 (288 games in 4 056–4 763 s
  contended). A 4× sims cut is a ≈ 4× cut in the searching sides' NN work per ply; how much of the
  38 s follows it is a bench, not a census (the child's fixed costs — spawn, snapshot load, book —
  are the residue). Not measured here.
- **The sealbot rung DELETED unless it reads something strix does not.** It read 0.657 ± 0.045 flat
  over run7 while strix swung 0.045 → 0.170 → 0.111, and it did not separate promoted from rejected
  rounds (§(ii)). Deleting it retires its consumers with it: the `sealbot_wr_abort` row (DEFERRED,
  inert in run8), `sealbot_wr_warn`, the ladder state, `eval_channel_health`, `wr_sealbot` on
  `eval_round_complete`, the dashboard's series — R4/LAW-07 says each loses its producer and goes
  in the same commit. What the rung reads that strix does not: a book-v1 paired WR against a
  fixed CPU opponent at ≈ 45 min per round; the same 288-game budget in a strix cell reads the
  external series the run is judged on.
- **GSPRT H0 0.50 if (iii) holds.** The mechanism holds; the lever is −7 % pairs (≈ 1.3 h over run7)
  for a 2 % → 5 % false-promotion rate on an equal candidate (§(iii)). Not the wall.
- **If (i) says promotion selects nothing in the loop → the gate is an instrument.** It does
  (§(i)). The gate's live effect is the anchor for its own next comparison and the parent of the
  next run; at the cadence of the reading it costs one round per 15k.
