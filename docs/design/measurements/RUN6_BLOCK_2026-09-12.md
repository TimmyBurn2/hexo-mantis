A 32-game round resolves ± 15 pp, so single rounds are noise; the pooled trough at 10k–14k against
either neighbour is not (4/160 vs 26/160 and 25/160). The trough coincides with the policy-loss
peak and the value-loss plateau; the recovery coincides with the value loss falling to its block
minimum. The channel-health monitor labelled the rung MEASURING throughout (pooled 128-game
windows 5.5 % → 14.1 %, running max 20.3 %, never `degraded`).

**The promotion arena (deploy-matched, every third round):** promoted at **3 000**, rejected at
6 000 / 9 000 / 12 000 / 15 000 / 21 000, promoted at **18 000** and **24 000**. Three anchors in
the block; the final `best_model.pt` is step 24 000.

**How the candidate loses (800 rung games, 98 won, all decisive):** wins last 34 plies (median 31),
losses **28 plies (median 25)**, and **67 % of losses end inside 30 plies** — 12–15 compound turns
— while self-play games run 75 plies. Colour: 39/400 as first, 59/400 as second (a mild
second-side edge, inside noise). SealBot
plays a full-width, move-ordered alpha–beta to **5 compound turns** (its `max_depth` counts
turns; the adapter reads `last_depth` back as a receipt, so the depth is real) with a hand-written
threat evaluation; the candidate's search at 128 sims and `gumbel_m 16` reaches a **mean
simulation depth of 3.85 plies** (`iteration_complete.mcts_mean_depth`, self-play at mean 128
sims), i.e. under two turns, and its value head is 25 000 steps old. The losses are short forced
sequences the bot sees and the net does not — a tactical-horizon deficit, not the colony attractor
(colony fraction 0 in self-play) and not a strategic one.

**Scale, for the reader who asks "is 16 % bad":** the project's own history never exceeded
≈ 33 % on this bar (F-27: 33 % → 5 %; F-22: 8 → 12 → 2 %; PERF-3b's warm start: 28 % at step
200). In the literature a Gumbel run at n = 16 sims is trained on 10²–10³× more games than this
block's 25 000: MiniZero's 9×9 Go runs generate 2 000 games per 200 optimisation steps and are
evaluated every 3 000 steps (= 30 000 games) with 200 games at 400 sims against a fixed baseline;
AlphaGo Zero's evaluator ran every 1 000 steps of batch 2 048 (≈ 2 M positions and ≈ 7 000
games per interval, 7–8× this block's 1 000-step window). At one step per game the block saw each position ≈ 3.7 times through
the 100 k ring, which is inside the usual 1–4× reuse band — the budget is short in GAMES, not in
epochs.

## D. The eval cadence, measured, and what the literature does

Measured on this block: 25 rounds (17 screens of 56 games, 8 gate rounds of 136–264), 2 552 eval
games, **5.9 h of the 21.8 h wall with a round alive (27 %)** — but self-play ran at **0.94×**
during a round (steps/h 1 111 in-round vs 1 185 out), not the 0.79× measured before PERF-A4 at
16 workers (CARD-EVAL-CONTENTION), so the rounds cost **≈ 440 steps ≈ 1.7 % of the block ≈ 22
min of wall**. The cadence is no longer a wall-clock question. It is a SIGNAL question: a
32-game screen is ± 15 pp, the run's own monitor needs four rounds (128 games) to say anything,
and R334(f)(ii) asks for a CI that excludes 0.5 — unreachable from 32-game rounds at any WR under
≈ 0.7.

What the references do (sources at the foot):

| system | cadence | games per evaluation | gates self-play? |
|---|---|---|---|
| AlphaGo Zero (2017) | every 1 000 steps (batch 2 048) | 400 at 1 600 sims vs the current best, 55 % to promote | yes |
| AlphaZero (2018) | none — the latest net is deployed to self-play immediately | Elo measured off-line | no |
| KataGo | gatekeeper OPTIONAL; "passing 0 for USEGATING … will be faster and will save compute power, and the whole loop works perfectly fine without it" | 200 games, ≥ 100 wins | optional, off for speed |
| Gumbel MuZero / MuZero | none in the loop; Elo against fixed references on a schedule, off the training path | off-line | no |
| MiniZero (AZ / Gumbel AZ, 9×9 Go) | every 3 000 steps = 15 iterations = 30 000 games | 200 vs a fixed baseline at 400 sims | no |
| OpenSpiel alpha_zero | continuous evaluators vs vanilla MCTS at increasing sims, off the training path | — | no |

Read against run6: every reference evaluates **once per 10³–10⁴ games** with **≥ 200 games** per
point and (bar AlphaGo Zero) **does not gate**; run6 evaluates once per ≈ 1 000 games with 32,
and gates every third. The regime note this record owes (REPAIR-A4 remainder): at the block's
rate the cost of the cadence is 1.7 %, so the lever is not "less often" but "**fewer, larger
points**" — the same 2 500 games as 6–8 rounds of 300 would put ± 5 pp on each point and make
R334(f)(ii) statable. `train.eval_interval` stays R343(b)/R345's 1 000 until a ruling moves it;
the numbers above are the input to that ruling, not a change.
